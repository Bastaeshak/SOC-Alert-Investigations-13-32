# CLD-0228: Weekly alert volume report: elevated identity alerts

Engagement: Cloudora · Shift 7 · 2026-09-02
Verdict: False positive · Severity (my rating): Informational (reported Informational)
QA review: Excellent

## Alert
Scheduled identity-volume report identified identity alerts running at 3.1× the four-week average, primarily involving guest-account and executive-account rules between Wednesday and Monday. Endpoint and email alert volumes remained within normal ranges. The report requested review of the variance and whether identity detection thresholds require tuning.

## Hypothesis
Detection-noise hypothesis: The increase was caused by overly sensitive identity rules generating unnecessary alerts, indicating threshold tuning may be required.

Legitimate-volume hypothesis: The increase reflects the genuine increase in malicious identity activity associated with this week's Bexley incident, meaning the elevated alert volume is expected and the rules are functioning as intended.

## Evidence checked
The report provides no independent raw telemetry and specifically relies on this week's ticket history. Therefore, the investigation was based on the identity alerts already investigated during the week rather than attempting to infer additional events from unavailable evidence.

Review of this week's investigations showed multiple confirmed malicious identity-related events associated with the ongoing Bexley compromise, including activity involving compromised/targeted accounts and known malicious infrastructure.

The increased alert volume therefore corresponds with a real increase in malicious identity activity, rather than currently demonstrating that the identity rules are excessively sensitive.

The alert history should still be reviewed for unnecessary duplicate detections, repeated False Positives, or opportunities to narrow individual rules without reducing coverage of the malicious activity observed this week.

## Action
No incident containment is required for this scheduled report because it does not identify a new compromise or previously unhandled malicious event.

Continue reviewing the identity alerts contributing to the 3.1× increase and compare their TP/FP/duplicate outcomes. Only tune individual rules if repeated noise or unnecessary duplicate detection can be demonstrated. Do not broadly raise identity thresholds solely because alert volume increased during a week with confirmed elevated malicious identity activity.

Continue monitoring identity-alert volume after the Bexley incident stabilizes to determine whether it returns toward the four-week baseline.

## Justification
The scheduled report is assessed as False Positive / Informational because the increased identity-alert volume is consistent with the genuine increase in malicious identity activity investigated during the Bexley incident. The report itself does not identify a new malicious event; it flags an aggregate variance for analyst review.

The 3.1× increase alone does not demonstrate that the detection thresholds are too sensitive. This week's ticket history contains multiple legitimate security detections associated with the active incident, providing a reasonable explanation for the elevated identity-alert volume. Endpoint and email volumes remaining within their normal ranges does not change the fact that the week's incident activity was concentrated heavily in identity-related detections.

No additional raw evidence was supplied with this report beyond the ticket history, so no unsupported telemetry conclusions were made.

## Tuning note
No broad threshold tuning recommended at this time. The increased identity-alert volume appears to reflect genuine malicious activity rather than demonstrated rule sensitivity.

Review the contributing guest-account and executive-account rules individually for repeated False Positives or duplicate detection paths. Any tuning should be narrow and evidence-based so that detection coverage for the Bexley activity is preserved. Reassess alert volume after the active incident period to determine whether identity detections return toward their normal baseline.
