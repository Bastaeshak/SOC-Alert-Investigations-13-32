# CLD-0220: Inbound mail flood: finance@cloudora.io

Engagement: Cloudora · Shift 5 · 2026-08-30
Verdict: True positive · Severity (my rating): Medium (reported Low)
QA review: Good

## Alert
Mid-shift alert - Inbound volume anomaly

Rule "Inbound volume anomaly" fired after finance@cloudora.io received 380 messages between 07:00 and 07:30 UTC.

The messages consisted of newsletter and account-confirmation emails from legitimate services that the finance mailbox had never subscribed to. The activity matches a subscription-bomb pattern in which large volumes of legitimate subscription emails are generated to flood a victim's mailbox.

## Hypothesis
Malicious hypothesis:
An attacker deliberately subscription-bombed finance@cloudora.io using legitimate services to overwhelm the mailbox and potentially conceal a finance-related message or fraudulent activity within the flood.

Benign hypothesis:
The sudden volume may have resulted from unsolicited or accidental subscription activity without a separate malicious objective or hidden finance-related action.

## Evidence checked
- Reviewed the supplied subscription-flood email sample received by finance@cloudora.io at 07:04:19 UTC, within the reported 07:00-07:30 UTC flood.

- The sample was a subscription-confirmation email from Garden Club Weekly. SPF, DKIM and DMARC all passed, supporting that the message originated from the legitimate service rather than a spoofed sender.

- The evidence states this was one of 380 similar messages received during the 30-minute period and that finance@cloudora.io had not subscribed to these services.

- Compared the activity against the Shift 5 intel digest. The brief specifically identifies subscription bombing as a technique used to flood a mailbox and potentially bury payment-change or invoice-related messages.

- Searched available KQL telemetry for finance@cloudora.io and finance-related terms in an attempt to identify related activity. No current October 9 message or action associated with the flood was identified.

- A historical August inbox-rule event involving daniel.reeve@cloudora.io and finance/invoice messages was returned during the search. This predates the current incident and no technical evidence connects it to the October 9 subscription bomb, so it was excluded from the current incident assessment.

- Reviewed the available email evidence. The only other supplied email was the historical October 6 Bexley phishing message to marcus.oje@cloudora.io. No evidence establishes that message as related to the October 9 subscription flood.

- The subscription bomb itself is supported by the available evidence, but the specific message or activity it may have been intended to conceal could not be identified with the available telemetry.

## Action
Treat the subscription bomb as confirmed malicious activity and continue monitoring finance@cloudora.io for related fraud or account activity.

Recommend reviewing mail-flow/message-trace data for the 07:00-07:30 UTC flood, expanding the window as needed, to identify any payment-change, invoice, supplier, account-security, or other business-relevant message that may have been buried.

Notify the finance team of the subscription bomb and advise them to carefully review messages received during the flood before acting on payment, supplier, or account-change requests.

Flag the activity to the vCISO as threat intelligence due to the deliberate targeting of the finance mailbox and its match to the current subscription-bomb intelligence.

No formal escalation at this time because no underlying fraud, account compromise, client/payroll-data access, or other escalation trigger has been confirmed.

## Justification
Reviewed the supplied subscription-flood email evidence.

Email evidence:
Received: 2026-10-09 07:04:19 UTC
From: welcome@garden-club-news.example
To: finance@cloudora.io
Subject: Confirm your subscription
SPF: Pass
DKIM: Pass
DMARC: Pass

The message was received during the reported 07:00-07:30 UTC anomaly and was identified as one of 380 similar newsletter/account-confirmation messages received by finance@cloudora.io. The mailbox had not subscribed to the services.

Passing SPF, DKIM and DMARC supports that legitimate services were generating the subscription messages rather than the messages simply being spoofed.

The activity was compared against the Shift 5 intel digest, which specifically identifies subscription bombing as a technique used to flood a mailbox and potentially bury payment-change or invoice-related messages.

KQL search performed:

search "finance@cloudora.io"

Result:
No current October 9 finance-mailbox activity relevant to the subscription bomb was identified. The search returned a historical 2026-08-10 inbox-rule event involving daniel.reeve@cloudora.io. Because no technical evidence connects that historical event to the current October 9 activity, it was excluded from the incident assessment.

Additional broad searches for invoice/payment-related activity did not identify a current message associated with the flood.

The other available email evidence was the historical October 6 Bexley phishing email sent to marcus.oje@cloudora.io. No evidence establishes that email as the message concealed by the October 9 subscription bomb.

Verdict: True Positive.
Severity: Medium.

The subscription bomb itself is confirmed malicious activity targeting the finance mailbox. However, the available evidence does not identify a specific fraudulent message concealed by the flood or establish account compromise, payment fraud, or access to client/payroll data. Therefore, the activity does not currently meet the formal escalation SOP.

Medium severity reflects the confirmed deliberate targeting and disruption of a finance mailbox, combined with the potential for the flood to conceal financially significant activity. The activity should also be flagged to the vCISO as useful threat intelligence.

## Tuning note
No broad suppression recommended. The inbound-volume rule correctly identified an abnormal subscription flood.

Consider enriching future alerts by correlating sudden inbound-volume spikes with unusual invoice, payment-change, supplier, account-security, or other finance-related messages received during the same time window. Legitimate subscription senders should not be broadly blocked or suppressed because they are being abused to generate the flood rather than acting as the attacker.
