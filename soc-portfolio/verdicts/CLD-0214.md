# CLD-0214: Payroll export emailed to personal address: marcus.oje

Engagement: Cloudora · Shift 4 · 2026-08-29
Verdict: Insufficient data · Severity (my rating): Medium (reported High)
QA review: Needs Improvement

## Alert
DLP detected marcus.oje@cloudora.io sending payroll_reconciliation_sept.csv, containing 2,140 rows with salary fields, from his corporate mailbox to moje.home@personal-mail.example. Subject was "for weekend".

## Hypothesis
Malicious: Marcus intentionally transferred sensitive payroll data to a personal account without authorization.
Benign: Marcus sent the file to himself to work over the weekend but used an unapproved method to do so.

## Evidence checked
Sign-in baseline

Marcus successfully signed in at 08:20:47 UTC.
Source: 203.0.113.10, London UK.
Device: Windows/registered.
MFA satisfied.
Sign-in was marked usual session.
The payroll email was sent at 08:21 UTC.
No evidence of anomalous authentication or account compromise was identified.

Helpdesk and shift context

Reviewed helpdesk records for Marcus, payroll exports, personal email use, and weekend-work exceptions.
No documented approval or exception was found for this transfer.
Shift handover also contains no authorization for Marcus to send payroll information to a personal mailbox.

KQL/database searches

Searched for marcus.oje@cloudora.io, moje.home@personal-mail.example, and payroll_reconciliation_sept.csv.
Marcus's normal sign-in was returned, but no additional evidence explaining or authorizing the transfer was identified.
Searches for the personal-mail domain and payroll filename returned no additional rows.

## Action
Contact Marcus and his manager to confirm why the payroll file was sent to his personal email and whether the transfer was authorized. Preserve the DLP evidence and verify whether the personal mailbox received the file. If the transfer was unauthorized or malicious, escalate immediately because payroll data is involved.

Recommended future control: Review DLP/email policies and consider blocking or requiring additional approval for outbound emails containing sensitive payroll data when the destination is an external or personal email address. This would help prevent similar data transfers rather than only alerting after they occur.

## Justification
The DLP alert confirms that Marcus sent payroll_reconciliation_sept.csv, containing 2,140 payroll records with salary fields, from his corporate mailbox to his personal email address.

KQL was used to establish Marcus's sign-in baseline:

signin
| where User == "marcus.oje@cloudora.io"
| order by Timestamp asc

The search returned:

2026-10-08 08:20:47 UTC
User: marcus.oje@cloudora.io
Result: Success
IP: 203.0.113.10
Location: London UK
Device: Windows/registered
Client: Browser
MFA: Satisfied
Notes: usual session

This occurred immediately before the 08:21 UTC payroll email and supports that the activity came from Marcus's normal authenticated session rather than an obvious account compromise.

Additional database-wide KQL searches were performed for:

marcus.oje@cloudora.io
moje.home@personal-mail.example
payroll_reconciliation_sept.csv
personal-mail.example

The Marcus search returned only his normal sign-in activity. Searches for the personal email/domain and payroll filename returned no additional rows. Helpdesk and shift context were also reviewed, and no documented approval or exception was found allowing Marcus to send payroll data to a personal mailbox.

The subject "for weekend" provides a possible legitimate explanation, but this does not prove the transfer was authorized. Because the sensitive payroll transfer is confirmed but Marcus's intent and authorization cannot be established with the available evidence, the verdict is Insufficient Data. Marcus and his manager should be contacted for verification. If the transfer is confirmed unauthorized or malicious, the payroll-data escalation trigger must be applied.

## Tuning note
No detection tuning recommended. The DLP rule correctly identified sensitive payroll data being sent to an external personal address. As a preventive control, consider strengthening DLP policy to block or require approval for outbound payroll data sent to personal/external email addresses, rather than only generating an alert after the transfer.
