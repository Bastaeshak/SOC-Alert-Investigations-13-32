# CLD-0226: Scheduled task created by service account: LDN-SRV-APP1

Engagement: Cloudora · Shift 7 · 2026-09-02
Verdict: True positive · Severity (my rating): Low (reported High)
QA review: Needs Improvement

## Alert
A scheduled task named BexleySyncHealth on LDN-SRV-APP1 was observed polling bexley-sync.example (192.0.2.150). Proxy telemetry showed task-associated POST attempts at 02:58 UTC and 06:58 UTC on Oct 13, exactly four hours apart. Both attempts were blocked by the proxy.

## Hypothesis
Malicious: BexleySyncHealth is a persistence mechanism associated with the existing Bexley compromise and continued executing after earlier containment actions.

Benign: The task is an authorized component of the legitimate Bexley payroll integration.

## Evidence checked
We can start this with the strongest evidence we've established:

Proxy telemetry confirmed LDN-SRV-APP1 previously communicated with bexley-sync.example / 192.0.2.150 during CLD-0217.
Week 2 proxy telemetry showed BexleySyncHealth polling the same infrastructure at 02:58 and 06:58 UTC Oct 13; both attempts were blocked.
Bexley's security notification identifies bexley-sync.example as staging infrastructure and 192.0.2.150 as staging/collection infrastructure associated with the compromise.
The legitimate Bexley payroll integration uses SFTP to transfer.bexleypay.example, separating the legitimate integration from this activity.
Environment-wide proxy scoping found the suspicious domain associated only with LDN-SRV-APP1.
Searches of CloudoraAudit_CL returned no records confirming the scheduled-task creation, so the creation mechanism could not be independently verified from available audit telemetry.
Helpdesk records state APP1 was seized for forensics on Oct 12, yet task-associated proxy attempts were recorded from APP1 on Oct 13. This discrepancy should be documented rather than ignored.

## Action
Contain APP1 now: isolate LDN-SRV-APP1 from normal network access while preserving it for forensics. Ensure outbound access to bexley-sync.example / 192.0.2.150 remains blocked.
Remove/disable the persistence: disable BexleySyncHealth, but preserve the task definition/script and related artifacts first so forensic evidence isn't destroyed.
Resolve the containment discrepancy: determine why HD-5201 says APP1 was seized/offline on Oct 12 when proxy telemetry shows BexleySyncHealth attempts from APP1 on Oct 13 at 02:58 and 06:58 UTC.
APP2 recovery: verify LDN-SRV-APP2 was built cleanly under CHG-2110 before moving payroll operations to it. Then restore only the documented legitimate Bexley SFTP integration to transfer.bexleypay.example.
Endpoint evidence: request endpoint/EDR or Windows telemetry for APP1. Specifically, we want the scheduled-task definition, creation/modification time, creator/security context, PowerShell command/script executed by the task, parent/child processes, files created/downloaded, persistence artifacts, and any other network connections.
Service-account investigation: identify exactly which service account runs BexleySyncHealth, review its authentication/activity, determine whether its credentials could be compromised, and rotate them if compromise is suspected/confirmed. Also check where else that account is used before rotation so you don't break legitimate services.
Persistence scope: search other endpoints for BexleySyncHealth, the associated PowerShell/script content, Bexley's malicious hashes, bexley-sync.example, and 192.0.2.150. We can't do the endpoint portion with our current KQL schemas, so this requires endpoint/EDR/task-scheduler evidence.
IOC sweep: Bexley gave us additional indicators: 192.0.2.146, .203, .150, .88, docusign-verify.example, bexley-docs.example, plus the three hashes. Scope those across whatever telemetry supports them.
Monitor containment: continue monitoring APP1/service-account activity and the malicious IOCs. If APP1 generates additional traffic after confirmed isolation, that becomes an important containment failure to investigate.
Bexley notification: their Oct 13 notice asks customers to confirm sweep results within 48 hours. Cloudora has actually observed their indicators, so that finding should be passed through the incident/vCISO process rather than reporting a clean sweep.

## Justification
CLD-0226 was investigated as a potential persistence mechanism on LDN-SRV-APP1. Both malicious and benign hypotheses were considered. The malicious hypothesis was that BexleySyncHealth was persistence associated with the prior Bexley compromise; the benign hypothesis was that it was an authorized component of the legitimate Bexley payroll integration.

KQL Query 1 – Verify scheduled-task evidence in audit telemetry

Purpose: Determine whether BexleySyncHealth or related scheduled-task creation could be independently verified in CloudoraAudit_CL.

CloudoraAudit_CL
| where TimeGenerated between (datetime(2026-10-08) .. datetime(2026-10-11))
| where ActivityDisplayName contains "task"
   or Details contains "task"
   or Details contains "scheduled"
| project TimeGenerated, ActivityDisplayName, InitiatedBy, TargetUser, IPAddress, Result, Details
| order by TimeGenerated asc

Result: No rows returned. A separate tenant-wide search for BexleySyncHealth also returned no rows. Therefore, scheduled-task creation could not be independently verified through available CloudoraAudit_CL telemetry. This limitation was retained rather than assuming creation details.

KQL Query 2 – Scope suspicious Bexley infrastructure

Purpose: Determine which hosts communicated with bexley-sync.example and establish the first/last observed activity, request count, and outbound volume.

proxy
| where dest_domain =~ "bexley-sync.example"
| summarize
    FirstSeen=min(timestamp),
    LastSeen=max(timestamp),
    Requests=count(),
    TotalBytesOut=sum(bytes_out)
    by host, dest_ip, method
| order by FirstSeen asc

Result: Only LDN-SRV-APP1 was observed communicating with bexley-sync.example (192.0.2.150) using POST. Activity ranged from 2026-10-09 02:41:03 UTC through 2026-10-13 06:58:00 UTC. No other host was identified communicating with this domain in the available proxy telemetry.

KQL Query 3 – Review individual callback events

Purpose: Examine individual connections to the suspicious infrastructure and determine whether activity continued after the previous incident response.

proxy
| where dest_domain =~ "bexley-sync.example"
| project timestamp, host, dest_domain, dest_ip, method, bytes_out, notes
| order by timestamp asc

Result: Proxy telemetry showed the original CLD-0217 activity from APP1 on Oct 9, including the first and last requests of a reported 214-request/~41 MB sequence. On Oct 13 at 02:58 UTC, APP1 attempted another POST to bexley-sync.example; the proxy note identified it as a BexleySyncHealth task poll blocked at proxy CLD-0226. A second blocked poll occurred at 06:58 UTC, exactly four hours later. No successful Oct 13 callback was observed.

The benign hypothesis was further weakened by comparison with known legitimate activity. The documented Bexley payroll feed uses SFTP to transfer.bexleypay.example, whereas BexleySyncHealth attempted POST connections to bexley-sync.example. Bexley's Oct 13 security notification independently identified bexley-sync.example as malicious staging infrastructure and 192.0.2.150 as staging/collection infrastructure associated with the compromise.

Helpdesk context was also checked. HD-5201 states APP1 was seized for forensics and payroll middleware taken offline on Oct 12, while HD-5205 documents APP2 being built as its replacement on Oct 13. This conflicts with raw proxy telemetry showing task-associated attempts from APP1 on Oct 13 at 02:58 and 06:58 UTC. The discrepancy requires follow-up to determine whether APP1 was actually isolated as documented.

Verdict: True Positive — Low. Malicious persistence-related activity is confirmed with high confidence, but the observed Oct 13 outbound callbacks were blocked at the proxy with no successful connection shown, limiting the current alert's impact. Recommend isolating APP1, preserving and disabling BexleySyncHealth, obtaining endpoint/EDR and Task Scheduler evidence to identify its creation and execution chain, investigating the Oct 12 containment discrepancy, scoping the task/IOCs across endpoints, reviewing and rotating the associated service-account credential if warranted, validating APP2 as clean before restoring the legitimate Bexley SFTP integration, and monitoring for further activity.

## Tuning note
No detection tuning recommended. The alert correctly identified continued BexleySyncHealth activity communicating with known malicious Bexley infrastructure.
