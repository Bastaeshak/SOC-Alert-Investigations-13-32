# CLD-0229: Supplier breach notification with indicator list: Bexley Payroll Integrations

Engagement: Cloudora · Shift 8 · 2026-09-04
Verdict: True positive · Severity (my rating): Medium (reported High)
QA review: Good

## Alert
Bexley Payroll Integrations issued breach notification BXL-SEC-2026-11 after confirming compromise of Eleanor Walsh's workstation and mailbox. Bexley supplied 10 IOCs and requested Cloudora confirm sweep results within 48 hours.

## Hypothesis
Malicious: Bexley's newly supplied IOCs may identify additional Cloudora users or systems affected by the same Bexley-linked campaign that were missed during previous investigations.

Benign: IOC matches may represent previously investigated/contained activity, or isolated contact without successful compromise or follow-on malicious activity.

## Evidence checked
Swept the 4 supplied IPs and 3 domains across available Cloudora sign-in and proxy telemetry for 2026-10-06 00:00 UTC–2026-10-14 23:59 UTC.
Known IOC matches correlated with previously investigated malicious activity involving Eleanor Walsh, ops-admin, Daniel Reeve, Marcus Oje, and LDN-SRV-APP1.
Found a new IOC hit at 2026-10-09 08:12:44 UTC: LDN-WS-118, associated with chloe.iyer, made a single GET request to confirmed second-stage lure bexley-docs.example (192.0.2.151).
Searched all available proxy history for bexley-docs.example; no other Cloudora contacts were identified.
Scoped Chloe/LDN-WS-118 across proxy, signin, CloudoraSignIn_CL, CloudoraAudit_CL, and av_events. No additional sign-in, audit, AV, or follow-on proxy activity was identified.
Searched the available database for the supplied SHA256 fragments. No matches were found; however, Bexley supplied truncated hashes, preventing an exact SHA256 comparison.



## Action
Report the IOC sweep results and the Oct 6–14 UTC search window to Bexley within the 48-hour deadline, including the newly identified bexley-docs.example contact from LDN-WS-118. Block bexley-docs.example and its observed IP 192.0.2.151 at the perimeter/proxy and add both to the watchlist. Continue hunting for additional second-stage phishing infrastructure because the current intelligence indicates the operator may use separate document-lure domains not included in the original IOC set. Flag the Chloe/LDN-WS-118 finding to the vCISO as threat intelligence and obtain additional email, browser, or endpoint telemetry to determine how the workstation reached the lure, whether Chloe manually interacted with it, and whether any follow-on activity occurred. Request the full SHA256 hashes from Bexley and rerun the hash sweep once received. Re-raise/escalate if further investigation identifies credential compromise, malware execution, persistence, additional affected hosts/users, or access to client/payroll data.

## Justification
The alert is a True Positive, Medium severity because the Bexley supplier IOC sweep confirmed previously known malicious campaign activity and identified one previously unknown Cloudora contact with Bexley’s confirmed second-stage phishing infrastructure. I searched the four supplied IPs across signin and CloudoraSignIn_CL for 2026-10-06 00:00 UTC through 2026-10-14 23:59 UTC. The signin results correlated 192.0.2.146, 192.0.2.203, and 192.0.2.88 with previously investigated malicious activity involving Eleanor Walsh, ops-admin, Marcus Oje, and Daniel Reeve; CloudoraSignIn_CL returned no additional matches. I then searched proxy for all four supplied IPs and all three supplied domains during the same window. This correlated 192.0.2.150 and bexley-sync.example with the previously investigated APP1 activity and identified a new result at 2026-10-09 08:12:44 UTC, where LDN-WS-118, associated with chloe.iyer, made a single GET request to bexley-docs.example (192.0.2.151).

I expanded the investigation around the new finding. An unrestricted proxy search for bexley-docs.example showed that the Chloe/LDN-WS-118 request was the only occurrence in available proxy history. Searches for Chloe in both signin and CloudoraSignIn_CL returned no rows. Searches of CloudoraAudit_CL for Chloe returned no activity, a broader Oct 6–14 proxy search for LDN-WS-118 returned only the original request, and av_events contained no records for LDN-WS-118. Available telemetry therefore confirms contact with Bexley's malicious second-stage lure but does not establish credential compromise, malware execution, persistence, client/payroll-data access, or follow-on activity. The evidence also cannot establish whether Chloe manually clicked the lure or how the workstation reached the domain; additional email/browser/endpoint evidence is required.

Bexley's three SHA256 indicators were supplied only in truncated form (3f1c...a904, 91be...20d7, c07a...5518). I searched av_events and then the available database for the supplied fragments with no matches. Because the full 64-character SHA256 values were not provided, these results cannot conclusively establish absence of the malicious files, and the complete hashes must be obtained from Bexley for an exact sweep.
