# CLD-0222: Reported internal email: 'Updated payroll portal' from ewalsh.ext

Engagement: Cloudora · Shift 6 · 2026-08-31
Verdict: Escalate · Severity (my rating): High (reported Medium)
QA review: Good

## Alert
Three Cloudora staff reported an internal email sent from ewalsh.ext@cloudora.io at 21:14:37 UTC Friday, October 9, subject "Updated payroll portal, please re-authenticate." The email was sent to 12 Finance and HR recipients and directed them to 192.0.2.203/portal. Raw headers confirmed the message originated from an authenticated OWA session at 192.0.2.146.

## Hypothesis
Malicious hypothesis: The previously compromised ewalsh.ext account was used by the attacker to conduct second-stage internal phishing. The attacker leveraged a legitimate Cloudora account and current Bexley/payroll context to harvest another employee's authentication/session material and expand access.

Benign hypothesis: Eleanor legitimately sent an internal payroll portal update as part of the Bexley integration. This is contradicted by the message originating from standing watchlist IP 192.0.2.146, directing recipients to standing watchlist IP 192.0.2.203, and subsequent malicious activity against a recipient.

## Evidence checked
Reviewed 03_internal_portal_phish.eml, sign-in telemetry, proxy telemetry, Shift 6 handover/intelligence, and scoped both standing watchlist IOCs.

Raw email findings:

Sender: ewalsh.ext@cloudora.io
Authenticated internal message
Source session: 192.0.2.146
Sent: 21:14:37 UTC
12 Finance/HR recipients
Payroll/Bexley re-authentication lure
Destination: 192.0.2.203/portal

KQL - Identify access to phishing portal:

proxy
| where dest_ip == "192.0.2.203"
| project timestamp, host, dest_domain, dest_ip, method, bytes_out, notes
| order by timestamp asc

Finding: Daniel Reeve was the only confirmed recipient in available proxy telemetry to access the portal. His session issued a GET request to 192.0.2.203 at 21:36:08 UTC, approximately 22 minutes after phishing delivery.

KQL - Investigate Daniel after the click:

signin
| where Timestamp >= datetime(2026-10-09 21:30:00)
| where User contains "daniel.reeve"
| order by Timestamp asc

Findings:

05:52:31 UTC Saturday - daniel.reeve@cloudora.io had a successful token authentication from 192.0.2.146.
Infrastructure classified as unknown/hosting.
MFA recorded "Claim satisfied no prompt."
05:58:44 UTC - same account/IP had an active OWA session that created inbox rule . documented in CLD-0221.
Later authentication from 192.0.2.146 was blocked after incident controls became active.

This strongly supports compromise following Daniel's interaction with the phishing portal, although available telemetry does not directly record Daniel entering credentials.

KQL - Scope both watchlist IPs across accounts:

signin
| where IP in ("192.0.2.146", "192.0.2.203")
| order by Timestamp asc

Finding: Watchlist infrastructure was associated with authenticated activity involving multiple accounts. Eleanor had the known attacker session, Daniel had successful token authentication followed by mailbox manipulation, and Marcus Oje had a separate active OWA session from 192.0.2.146 associated with inbox-rule creation. Marcus's activity predates this phishing email and therefore is not attributed as a downstream CLD-0222 victim. Attempts against hr-manager and later attempts against affected accounts were blocked by Conditional Access.

## Action
Escalated to the vCISO - keep open pending containment and further investigation. Request revocation of active sessions/tokens for affected accounts and containment of confirmed compromised accounts. Preserve relevant mailbox and authentication evidence. Request/review mailbox audit logs for Daniel Reeve to determine additional attacker actions and identify any messages or sensitive data accessed or forwarded. Continue scoping 192.0.2.146 and 192.0.2.203 across available telemetry and investigate whether any of the other 11 phishing recipients show evidence of compromise. Correlate the separate Marcus Oje activity with CLD-0232 without assuming it originated from CLD-0222.

## Justification
At 2026-10-09 21:14:37 UTC, ewalsh.ext@cloudora.io sent the reported payroll re-authentication email from an authenticated OWA session at standing watchlist IP 192.0.2.146. Raw headers in 03_internal_portal_phish.eml confirmed the authenticated sender/session and the destination 192.0.2.203/portal. The message targeted 12 Finance/HR recipients.

Proxy telemetry was queried for destination IP 192.0.2.203. It identified daniel.reeve accessing the portal at 2026-10-09 21:36:08 UTC, approximately 22 minutes after delivery.

Sign-in telemetry for daniel.reeve@cloudora.io then showed Success (token) from 192.0.2.146 at 2026-10-10 05:52:31 UTC, from Unknown (hosting) infrastructure using Windows/Chrome. MFA recorded "Claim satisfied no prompt." At 05:58:44 UTC, the same account and IP had an active OWA session that created inbox rule . documented in CLD-0221.

IOC scoping of 192.0.2.146 and 192.0.2.203 across sign-in telemetry also identified separate authenticated activity involving marcus.oje@cloudora.io from 192.0.2.146 at 2026-10-08 23:57:12 UTC, associated with CLD-0232. Because this predates the CLD-0222 phishing email, it was not attributed as a downstream victim of this campaign.

The evidence confirms malicious activity affecting multiple accounts and successful downstream compromise following Daniel's interaction with the phishing portal. This meets the multi-account malicious activity escalation criterion and the standing order requiring immediate escalation for successful authentication involving either watchlist IP. Verdict: Escalate. Severity: High. The exact token-theft mechanism and extent of data exposure remain unconfirmed.

## Tuning note
No tuning recommended. The alert correctly identified a genuine internal phishing campaign. Consider maintaining detection coverage for authenticated internal messages containing known malicious/watchlist IOCs, as compromised legitimate accounts can be used for second-stage phishing.
