# CLD-0213: New OAuth consent: 'Bexley Sync Utility' granted by ewalsh.ext

Engagement: Cloudora · Shift 4 · 2026-08-29
Verdict: Escalate · Severity (my rating): Medium (reported Medium)
QA review: Excellent

## Alert
ewalsh.ext@cloudora.io granted consent to a new unverified application named Bexley Sync Utility. the app received Mail.REad, files,read,all, and offline_access permissions 

## Hypothesis
malicious: 
- the attacker used the app as persistence to keep access to elanor's email and files after the password was reset 

benign: 
- Elanor intentionally gave the app access to help with her work

## Action
Escalate to the vCISO and contain the suspected OAuth persistence. Revoke Bexley Sync Utility consent and associated tokens to remove its access to Eleanor's account. Investigate the application in a controlled/sandbox environment to confirm its behavior and determine what resources it attempted to access. If confirmed malicious, block the application and its associated domain/IP infrastructure. Review Eleanor's mailbox and file access for any activity performed through the granted permissions and continue monitoring the compromised guest account.

## Justification
The activity is assessed as malicious because ewalsh.ext was previously compromised and then granted a first-seen, unverified Bexley Sync Utility application Mail.Read, Files.Read.All, and offline_access. These permissions could allow persistent access to Eleanor's mail and files even after a password reset.

KQL review of signin established Eleanor's normal baseline as 198.51.100.77, London, macOS/Safari. The Oct 7 CLD-0209 activity instead came from 192.0.2.146, unknown hosting infrastructure, Windows/Chrome, with no prior history for that IP.

A database-wide KQL search for "Bexley Sync Utility" returned:

2026-10-08 23:52:40 UTC
host: ewalsh.ext session
dest_domain: login.cloudora.io
method: POST
bytes_out: 2810
notes: OAuth consent Bexley Sync Utility CLD-0213

This confirms an ewalsh.ext session performed the OAuth consent POST. Additional KQL searches for the app/domain, related IPs, mail/file access, exports, downloads, OAuth activity, and audit events did not identify evidence available to CLD-0213 showing exactly what resources the app accessed.

There is also a timestamp discrepancy: the alert reports Wednesday at 23:52 UTC, while the raw proxy record shows Oct 8 at 23:52:40 UTC.

Because the activity is strongly assessed as malicious but the available telemetry cannot determine what mail/files were accessed or fully bound the OAuth persistence, the incident meets the SOP requirement for Escalate. Medium severity is assigned because malicious persistent access was established, but actual data access/exfiltration has not yet been confirmed.

## Tuning note
No tuning recommended. The rule correctly detected a first-seen application from an unverified publisher requesting sensitive OAuth permissions. This behavior should continue to alert, especially when offline_access is requested.
